# Five-minute speaking script

Ten slides, 300 seconds total. Keep example and proposed-experiment qualifiers audible.

| Slide | Time | Suggested narration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Cover | 20s | Scouty helps founders and small B2B teams find prospects, write personal outreach and move interested buyers toward meetings. Start with your website. |
| 2 · Problem | 30s | Knowing what you sell is different from knowing who needs it next. Without an SDR team, founders coordinate research, contact discovery, writing, follow-ups and scheduling themselves. This is the problem we are designing for, not a quantified customer study. |
| 3 · Customer | 25s | Our starting customer is a founder or small team selling to other businesses without dedicated sales development. They have a product and a website, but may not have a lead list or a repeatable outreach workflow. |
| 4 · Solution | 30s | Scouty connects three jobs: finding better-fit prospects, starting relevant conversations and moving interest toward a meeting. The first email is reviewed before sending; the tool supports the founder rather than promising automatic sales. |
| 5 · Workflow | 40s | Scouty researches your company, maps competitors and suggests campaigns. You choose an audience, explore matching companies and add decision makers. It then helps draft personal emails, track replies and book interested people through a connected calendar. Work is saved as a project. |
| 6 · Example | 40s | This is an illustrative campaign, not a customer result. Imagine a privacy-first analytics business contacting a growing SaaS team. The shortened sample email connects clearer funnel insights with lighter tracking. Buyer context gives the email a reason to exist. The founder can review and edit the draft before sending. A reply is an opportunity, not a guaranteed meeting. |
| 7 · Differentiation | 25s | The positioning is a connected workflow rather than a collection of handoffs. Company and campaign context travels into outreach. We are not claiming exclusive features or a proven moat; pilots need to show whether the connected experience is valuable. |
| 8 · Experiments | 35s | The proposed first channel is founder communities and accelerator cohorts. Measure the path from approved email to interested reply, meeting and repeat use. A subscription with usage limits is a hypothesis to test, not announced pricing. |
| 9 · Readiness | 30s | The public product describes research, outreach, reply tracking and calendar booking. We are not presenting unverified user or revenue numbers. The next proof is pilot activation, buyer response, meetings, repeat use and willingness to pay. |
| 10 · Ask | 25s | We want introductions to small B2B teams willing to pilot Scouty, accelerator support with customer discovery and pilot design, and relevant early-stage investor connections. Try Scouty at scouty.to: start with your website, without an account needed to explore. |

## Questions to prepare for

**How is this different from existing sales tools?** The hypothesis is that connected company context and a guided workflow reduce founder coordination work. We need pilot evidence to quantify the difference.

**What traction do you have?** This deck does not provide verified customer metrics. Share a dated, defined cohort only after the team supplies evidence.

**What do you charge?** Pricing and unit economics are still hypotheses in this presentation. The proposed pilot includes willingness-to-pay research.

**Does Scouty guarantee meetings?** No. Booking depends on interested replies, calendar availability and project configuration; product capability is distinct from measured conversion.
